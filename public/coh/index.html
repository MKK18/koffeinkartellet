US Forces

  1. 01  Skip the Motor Pool. Go Barracks into Tank Depot.

    You build three production buildings. The best USF opener builds two, and 86% of those games stop there. Your Greyhound turns up at 7:15 and shoves your first Sherman out to 16:00. Cut it and your armour lands roughly where the Greyhound used to. Treat this as the best bet on the table, not a settled fact: the line we are pointing you at is four times rarer than the one we are telling you to drop, and at least one other advisor recommends the opposite. Read the risk before you commit.

    Evidence: USF vs population clock · USF tech timeline · USF openers · USF reference build

  2. 02  Convert to 76mm the moment your first Sherman is out.

    You do it at 28 minutes, and only in 2 of 9 games. Good players convert straight after the Tank Depot, in 164 of the top follow-ups. You are driving stock Shermans for most of every game.

    Evidence: USF tech timeline · USF reference build

  3. 03  Two riflemen, then upgrade. Not five.

    You make five before your first upgrade. Good USF players make two, and almost none go past three. You also reach a vehicle having bought nothing, where they have two upgrades down.

    Evidence: USF sequencing · USF vs population clock · USF reference build

  4. 04  Stop taking fights you are already losing.

    You die 4.25 times a minute in losses against 2.89 in wins, and your kill/death halves. This is the only thing that shows up in every single cut of your games, whatever mode or faction you filter to.

    Evidence: USF win conditions · by mode check

  5. 05  Quit spamming capture orders when it goes badly.

    74 capture orders in losses against 48 in wins, and only one in five sticks against one in three. You also move your rally point twice as often. Fewer orders, better chosen.

    Evidence: USF behaviour · USF win conditions

  6. 06  Get BARs early or skip them.

    150 manpower and 40 fuel at 17:44, by which point your tanks are already out and want the same fuel. Good players take their first upgrade at 3:10.

    Evidence: USF upgrades · USF vs population clock

  7. 07  Drop Armored. It is your default and it is the weakest pick you have.

    You take it in 44% of your USF games. It is below average in our 2v2 table at 49.3% and dead last among players at your rating at 52.0%, and it keeps sliding as rating climbs. Both tables agree, which is rare. Airborne and Special Operations are the safe upgrades, better in both tables. Advanced Infantry is the interesting one: near the bottom of our 2v2 table but top two at your rating over 4,306 games. Worth a few deliberate games.

    Evidence: USF battlegroups · USF battlegroups at your rating · suggested USF build

  8. 08  Do not chase territory on USF.

    Captures are slightly higher in your USF losses, 0.58 a minute against 0.51. On British it is the other way round and holding ground genuinely wins games. USF is where you win by trading well, so grabbing more points is not the lever there.

    Evidence: USF win conditions · USF behaviour

British

  1. 01  Get the Vickers down in the first minute.

    Yours arrives at 1:55 and your first team weapon at 3:15. Good British players have one down by 20 seconds, and almost all of them by 2:51. You win British games by holding ground, so this is the worst thing to be late on.

    Evidence: British vs population clock · British tech timeline · British win conditions

  2. 02  Your Company Command Post is 7 to 17 minutes late.

    Good players have it at 13:51, nearly all of them by 15:09. You get there at 22:08 on Matildas and 31:26 on Grants. British tech is a fixed ladder, so everything above that rung is waiting on you.

    Evidence: British vs population clock · British tech timeline

  3. 03  Drop Australian Defense. Keep Indian Artillery.

    Australian Defense is bad at every rating: 46.6% in 2v2 and 52.0% among players at your level, near the floor both ways. You have played it three times for 0-2. Indian Artillery is the one we got wrong. It looks poor in the all-ratings 2v2 table at 47.5%, but among 1400 to 1600 players it is 55.3% over 3,630 games, comfortably mid-table, and your own record with it is 2-0. Heavy Armor and Air and Sea are the two strongest at your level and you have barely touched either.

    Evidence: British battlegroups · British battlegroups at your rating

  4. 04  Keep your opener. Fix minute four onwards.

    Section Upgrades is the second-best British line and you play it 56% of the time. The pick is right; your record on it is 21 points under par. That is execution, not selection.

    Evidence: British lines

  5. 05  Build tanks on British.

    Three of your ten British games had no vehicle at all, and you lose zero vehicles in the ones you win. Armour is part of how this faction wins for you.

    Evidence: British tech timeline · British win conditions

  6. 06  More buildings is right on British and wrong on USF.

    Your British wins use four production buildings against three in losses. USF is the reverse: two in wins, three and a half in losses. British is a ladder you climb, USF is a menu you should not over-order from. Do not carry one lesson to the other.

    Evidence: British behaviour · British tech timeline · USF behaviour

Both factions

  1. 01  British is your better team faction, not USF.

    9-9 on British against 4-6 on USF in team games. Your 60% overall is a 1v1 number: in team games you are 14-16. The old reading had USF ahead and that was 1v1 doing the work.

    Evidence: by mode check · British win conditions · USF win conditions

  2. 02  Get a mortar out before three minutes.

    Four of five wins had one in your first five builds. None of the five losses did. Only 21 of 58 good USF players build one at all, so this is your edge rather than the standard thing to do.

    Evidence: early mortar · mortar or howitzer · British build per battlegroup

  3. 03  Your losses are frantic, not lazy.

    You issue more commands per minute when you lose, on both factions: 44 against 41 on USF, 43 against 35 on British. More capture orders, more retreats, less to show for it. Idle time is near zero either way, so nothing about your losses is passive.

    Evidence: USF behaviour · British behaviour

  4. 04  Keep squads alive long enough to veteran up.

    This is the biggest single split in your 2v2 games and it had no entry here until now. In 2v2 wins your squads reach rank 4 with 7,400 experience; in losses rank 2 with 2,700. On USF at 2v2 it is rank 7.5 and 18,600 against 2.5 and 4,750. Veterans hit harder and die less, so losing squads early costs you the veterans that would have stopped you losing squads. It is a loop, and it is why damage output barely differs while trades collapse.

    Evidence: USF win conditions · British win conditions · by mode check

  5. 05  Their first tank at six minutes is what beats you, and it is not an AT problem.

    Their first real vehicle shows up at 9:44 in your wins and 6:38 in your losses, and you win 40% when it lands before 8:00 against 60% after. Your response is not the issue: counting tanks and AT rifles as well as guns, you have an answer +0:04 behind their tank in wins and +0:56 in losses, which is the same number. Being ready first does not help either. So the thing to fix is why they had fuel for a tank at six minutes, not how fast you answer it.

    Evidence: early enemy armour · USF win conditions · British win conditions

British lines

How far up the British ladder you climb, and what you buy there.

British buildings are not a choice of order. The ladder is forced: Barracks (cohdb's Section Command Post), then Motor Platoon (Platoon Command Post), then Armoured Platoon (Company Command Post). Every tech path in the population data starts Section then Platoon and none skips a rung. So a British line is two decisions, not an order: how far up the ladder you climb, and what you buy first when you get there. "Platoon Command, Humber" means you stopped at the Motor Platoon and took the Humber first. "Company Command" means you climbed to the Armoured Platoon. "Section Upgrades" means you branched to the Training Center instead of buying a vehicle. USF is the opposite: its four buildings are parallel, so there the order is a real choice.

USF openers

Which buildings, in which order, and how the field does with each.

USF buildings are parallel. Barracks, Weapon Support Center, Motor Pool and Tank Depot are independent, so which ones you build and in what order is a genuine choice. This is the one faction here where "opener" means an order.

Source cohdb bucket win rates  ·  Patch 2.5.3 (game is on 2.5.5)  ·  2v2, all ratings

What "opener" means in this document. It is cohdb's own bucket, not a definition of mine. For USF it is which production buildings you built and in what order, which is why the column below reads "buildings and order". For British there is only one possible building order, so the bucket is instead the rung of the ladder you reached plus the first classified purchase there; that plate is called British lines for that reason.

What I do not know. cohdb publishes neither its classification rules nor the time window it uses, so I inferred the scheme from the bucket names, the tech paths and the most-common sequences. The exact cut-off for what counts as "the first purchase", and whether it is a time limit or a step limit, is unknown. That uncertainty applies to every bucket win rate in this file.

PLATE 8

British lines

How far up the British ladder you climb, and what you buy there.

British buildings are not a choice of order. The ladder is forced: Barracks (cohdb's Section Command Post), then Motor Platoon (Platoon Command Post), then Armoured Platoon (Company Command Post). Every tech path in the population data starts Section then Platoon and none skips a rung. So a British line is two decisions, not an order: how far up the ladder you climb, and what you buy first when you get there. "Platoon Command, Humber" means you stopped at the Motor Platoon and took the Humber first. "Company Command" means you climbed to the Armoured Platoon. "Section Upgrades" means you branched to the Training Center instead of buying a vehicle. USF is the opposite: its four buildings are parallel, so there the order is a real choice.
